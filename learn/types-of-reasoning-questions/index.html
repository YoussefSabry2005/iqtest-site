The main types of reasoning test questions (with examples)

An overview of the question types used in IQ-style reasoning tests, from number series to syllogisms, with links to practice and strategy guides.

Number and letter series

A sequence follows a hidden rule and you supply the next term. Rules include constant steps, growing steps, multiplication and two interleaved sequences. Number series guide · letter series guide.

Number relationships

Pairs such as 3 → 10 and 5 → 26 share one rule (here, square and add one). You find it and apply it to a new number. Guide.

Matrix reasoning

A 3×3 grid of tiles with one missing. Each feature (shape, count and fill) follows its own rule across the rows. These are among the most widely used non-verbal reasoning items. Guide.

Mental rotation

You decide which option is the original shape rotated rather than mirrored. This measures your ability to manipulate images in your mind. Guide.

Syllogisms

Two statements about groups, and you choose the conclusion that must follow. Nonsense words keep real-world knowledge out of it. Guide.

Ordering puzzles

Comparisons such as "Ava is taller than Ben" that you combine into a full ranking. Guide.

Why tests mix question types

No single question type captures reasoning well on its own. A matrix rewards visual rule-finding; a syllogism rewards careful verbal logic; a number series rewards pattern search with numbers. Mixing several types, and ordering them from easy to hard, gives a more balanced picture and reduces the effect of being unusually good or bad at one format.
