IQ score ranges explained: what does a score of 100, 115 or 130 mean?

How the IQ scale is built, what share of people fall in each range, and why every score should be read as a range rather than an exact number.

The bell curve

IQ scores are designed to follow a normal distribution (a bell curve) with an average of 100 and a standard deviation of 15. Because of how normal distributions work, the share of people in each band is predictable.

How many people fall in each range

About 68% of people score within one standard deviation of the average, between 85 and 115. About 95% score between 70 and 130. Only around 2% score above 130, and around 2% below 70. These percentages come from the mathematics of the bell curve, so they apply to any test built on this scale.

Percentiles

A percentile tells you the share of the reference group you scored higher than. A score of 100 is the 50th percentile; 115 is roughly the 84th; 130 is roughly the 98th. On this site, percentiles are only shown once enough people have taken the test to make them meaningful.

Every score has a margin of error

No test measures anyone perfectly. A score is best read as a range: if you took a different version of the test on a different day, your result would probably move by a few points. Professional reports usually give a confidence interval, and this site shows a 95% range next to every result for the same reason.

Labels vary between tests

Different test publishers use different descriptive labels for ranges (such as "average" or "high average"), and the cut-offs are not universal. The numbers themselves are more informative than the labels, and neither should be over-interpreted.
