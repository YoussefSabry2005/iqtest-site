Puzzle 1

Assume both statements are true, and that each group has at least one member.

1. Some pims are not fleps.

2. No quans are fleps.

Which conclusion must be true?

ANone of these conclusions must be true.
BAll quans are pims.
CSome quans are pims.
DSome pims are quans.
EAll pims are quans.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A

The statements don't link pims and quans tightly enough: for every listed conclusion there is a situation where both statements are true but the conclusion is false.

How to approach this type

Syllogisms give two statements about groups and ask which conclusion must be true. The key word is “must”: a conclusion that is merely possible is wrong.

Ignore the meaning of the words

Questions use made-up words on purpose. Treat them as labels for groups (A, B, C) so real-world knowledge cannot mislead you.

Draw circles

Sketch each group as a circle. “All A are B” puts circle A inside B; “No A are B” keeps them apart; “Some A are B” means they overlap at least a little.

Puzzle 2

Assume both statements are true, and that each group has at least one member.

1. No blips are vosks.

2. All blips are trels.

Which conclusion must be true?

ASome vosks are trels.
BAll trels are vosks.
CSome trels are not vosks.
DSome trels are vosks.
ENone of these conclusions must be true.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C

Combining the statements through the shared group "blips" guarantees: Some trels are not vosks. None of the other options is forced by the statements; each can be false in some situation where both statements hold.
