How to solve syllogism (logical deduction) questions

Syllogisms give two statements about groups and ask which conclusion must be true. The key word is “must”: a conclusion that is merely possible is wrong.

Ignore the meaning of the words

Questions use made-up words on purpose. Treat them as labels for groups (A, B, C) so real-world knowledge cannot mislead you.

Draw circles

Sketch each group as a circle. “All A are B” puts circle A inside B; “No A are B” keeps them apart; “Some A are B” means they overlap at least a little.

Hunt for a counter-example

For each candidate conclusion, try to draw a picture where both statements are true but the conclusion is false. If you can, the conclusion does not have to be true.

Two classic traps

“All A are B” and “All C are B” says nothing about A and C. Two negative statements never lead to a definite conclusion.

Try one

Puzzle 1

Assume both statements are true, and that each group has at least one member.

1. All vosks are trels.

2. Some quans are vosks.

Which conclusion must be true?

ASome trels are quans.
BSome quans are not trels.
CNone of these conclusions must be true.
DSome trels are not quans.
ENo trels are quans.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A

Combining the statements through the shared group "vosks" guarantees: Some trels are quans. None of the other options is forced by the statements; each can be false in some situation where both statements hold.
