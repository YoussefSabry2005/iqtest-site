How to solve letter series questions

Letter series are number series in disguise. Convert letters to their positions in the alphabet (A = 1 … Z = 26) and the pattern usually becomes obvious.

Convert to numbers

Write the position under each letter: C, F, I, L becomes 3, 6, 9, 12, so the next letter is position 15 = O. Memorising the anchors E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20 makes this fast.

Watch for growing jumps

A, B, D, G, K moves +1, +2, +3, +4; the next jump is +5, giving P.

Alternating series

If neighbouring letters seem unrelated, split odd and even positions into two separate series and solve each.

Backward steps

Sequences can run backwards (Z, W, T, Q …). Negative steps work exactly the same way.

Common mistakes

  • Counting positions by hand every time. Learn anchors such as E = 5, J = 10, O = 15 and T = 20.
  • Off-by-one errors: moving 3 places from C lands on F, not G.
  • Assuming wrap-around (Z → A) when the series simply stops before Z.

A simple practice plan

Practise converting letters to numbers quickly, then solve the number pattern. Within a week, most people find letter series become as easy as number series.
