How to solve mental rotation questions

Mental rotation questions ask which option is the original shape rotated, as opposed to flipped (mirrored). Rotations keep a shape’s “handedness”; mirror images reverse it.

Pick a distinctive feature

Find an asymmetric detail, such as a single square sticking out on one side, and track where it goes as you rotate.

Rotate in quarter turns

Turn the shape 90° at a time in your head and compare. Four quarter turns bring you back to the start.

Check handedness

If the distinctive feature ends up on the opposite side relative to the rest of the shape, that option is a mirror image and can never match by rotation.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing the overall outline instead of tracking one distinctive square.
  • Mixing up clockwise and anticlockwise. Pick one direction and stick to it.
  • Forgetting that mirror images can look very similar after a rotation.

A simple practice plan

Try rotating real objects, such as a letter L cut from paper, and compare them with their mirror images. Physical practice makes the mental version noticeably easier.
